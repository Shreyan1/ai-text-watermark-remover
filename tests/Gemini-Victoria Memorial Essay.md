# The Victoria Memorial: The Crown Jewel of Kolkata

Standing majestically in the heart of Kolkata, the Victoria Memorial is one of India's most celebrated architectural landmarks. Commissioned by Lord Curzon, the Viceroy of India, following the demise of Queen Victoria in 1901, the monument was envisioned as a grand memorial and museum to commemorate her reign. Construction began in 1906, and the monument was officially opened to the public in 1921.

Designed by the renowned architect Sir William Emerson, the structure represents an exquisite blend of British and Mughal elements—a style known as Indo-Saracenic revivalist architecture. Built entirely from white Makrana marble sourced from Rajasthan (the very marble used in the Taj Mahal), the monument rises to a height of 184 feet. Atop its central dome stands the iconic sixteen-foot bronze statue of the "Angel of Victory," which acts as a weather vane and rotates with the wind.

Surrounded by 64 acres of meticulously manicured gardens, serene water bodies, and ornamental statues, the Victoria Memorial serves not only as a testament to historical craftsmanship but also as a peaceful green sanctuary in the bustling metropolis. Today, the memorial functions as an illustrious museum housing 25 galleries. Its extensive collection includes rare portraits of British royalty, historic paintings by Thomas and William Daniell, colonial artifacts, ancient manuscripts, and historical arms.

More than just a relic of the colonial past, the Victoria Memorial has transcended its imperial origins to become the defining cultural symbol of Kolkata—the "City of Joy." It attracts millions of visitors, historians, and tourists from around the world each year.
